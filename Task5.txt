Task 5: Strategy & Leadership (Short Answers)

1. You are leading a team of 4 QA engineers. The sprint velocity is dropping due to flaky automation tests. How will you handle this situation?

Ans. As a QA Lead, my first priority would be to stabilize automation without allowing flaky tests to block sprint progress or hide genuine failures.

I would approach this in five steps:

1. Identify flaky tests: Analyze CI history and identify tests that fail intermittently but pass on reruns. Temporarily quarantine them so they don't block merges, but continue monitoring their results.

2. Root Cause Analysis: Categorize failures into locator issues, hard-coded waits, test data dependencies, environment instability, and synchronization issues.

3. Team Ownership: Distribute fixes across the four engineers, assign a rotating flaky-test owner, and reserve 15–20% of sprint capacity for stabilization.

4. Prevent Recurrence: Introduce coding standards such as explicit waits, stable selectors (`data-testid`), independent test data, and repeated execution of new tests before merging.

5. Track Improvements: Monitor flaky test percentage, automation pass rate, rerun time, and regression execution time during sprint retrospectives.

My objective would be to make automation a reliable quality gate rather than a bottleneck, improving sprint predictability and team velocity.

2. During production release, you find 2 critical bugs reported by the client that were missed in QA. What’s your approach to fix gaps in process and testing?

Ans. I would handle this in two parallel tracks:

1. Immediate Resolution:
	- Assess business impact and affected users.
	- Coordinate with development for a hotfix, rollback, or feature flag if required.
	- Perform targeted testing and focused regression.
	- Keep the client and stakeholders informed.

2. Process Improvement:
	- Conduct a blameless RCA using the 5 Whys technique.
	- Identify whether the gap was in requirements, test coverage, test data, environment, or 		regression testing.
	- Add the missed scenarios to the regression suite.
	- Strengthen requirement reviews and risk-based testing.
	- Introduce mandatory release checklists, smoke testing, and post-release monitoring.

3. Measure Effectiveness:

	- Track defect leakage, production incidents, and recurring defect patterns over 		subsequent releases.

My objective would be to identify the process gaps, prevent recurrence, and move from reactive defect fixing to proactive quality assurance.
